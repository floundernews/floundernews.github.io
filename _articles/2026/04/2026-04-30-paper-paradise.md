In honour of Earth Day, the entire world has officially replaced every single object with paper. Yes, that’s right—paper: The thin sheet made of processed wood that everyone has collectively decided is now suitable for absolutely everything. Although this project has taken a while (only like 20 years), this decision is certainly a groundbreaking step towards sustainability.

On Wednesday morning, citizens woke up to a paper wonderland. Their beds were made out of paper. Their cars were made out of paper. Their house…you name it, paper\! The paper neighbourhood itself is a bit irregular, like failed origami, but some argue that it only adds character. Nearby, newly built paper apartments have impressed buyers with their modern, minimalistic design and natural ventilation, especially after the walls dissolved slightly during the drizzle. All it took was just one sneeze to wipe down an entire neighbourhood.

From then on, citizens have brushed their teeth with paper toothbrushes and drank water from paper water bottles. Bottles dissolve before the cap is even fully twisted off, and it becomes a fun little race against dehydration. The new, fully recycled paper cars are super lightweight and fold neatly in the paper parking spaces, but there have been reports about these cars collapsing or flying away during rush hour. Wow, flying cars really do exist in 2026\!

Luxury brands like Balenciaga are now selling paper jackets and paper footwear for only \$9999.99 and your kidneys; what a steal\! Despite their claim that their limited-edition paper raincoats are waterproof, their lifespan is approximately 10 seconds in Canada’s extreme weather. Maybe 15 seconds if we’re stretching it… Not only that, paper umbrellas have also become exceptionally popular\! Offering users a quick but unforgettable, soaked experience in the rain, they are so effective that they disintegrate within minutes of rain contact—perfect for hydration\!

This project might just be the greatest Earth Day achievement of the millennium. With plastic, glass, and metal removed from daily lives, the planet has experienced a major decrease in pollution. Every destroyed object can simply just be reused, reduced, and recycled. While there has been a 1000% increase in papercuts, citizens have already adapted to this paperific lifestyle, with paper umbrellas, paper clothing, and paper furniture becoming everyday essentials.. (At least until the weather forecast says otherwise...)
