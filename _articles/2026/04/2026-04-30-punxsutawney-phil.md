It seems that spring has, finally, sprung. The sun is shining, the birds are singing, flowers are poking out of the melting snow. Spring brings with it renewal, new life, and change. It makes one feel hopeful after a long, depressing winter. Given how important this season is to our wellbeing, wouldn’t it be great if it could arrive sooner? _Well, sure,_ I hear you say, _but that’s impossible. No one can control the climate._ You’re right. No person can turn the seasons. However, there is a groundhog who can, and he’s messing with us so he can coast by with the easiest job in the world. Beloved by all, but serving none, Punxsutawney Phil is scamming us all by working one day a year just to tell us something we already knew.

Groundhog Day is a tradition started in 1887 in the town of Punxsutawney of getting a groundhog (Phil) to look at his shadow to predict the end of winter. If he sees it (which he usually does), it means there will be six more weeks before spring is upon us. The way the ceremony is set up makes it seem as though Punxsutawney Phil is an immortal groundhog with weather-predicting powers. This simply isn’t true. He is, in fact, an immortal groundhog with weather-_controlling_ powers.

No one else seems to see it, but I know the truth. Phil is a groundhog who is able to control whether or not spring will come early and, 84% of the time, he makes sure it doesn’t. He does this even though he knows that people will be happier if spring comes sooner. Why does he do this? Well, he knows if he lets spring come sooner more often, people would be less concerned with what he had to say. By keeping us freezing longer, pining for warmth, he makes sure we hang on his every word (or, glance). By doing so, he ironically makes his predictions less useful. I know that winter will last 6 more weeks most of the time. I don’t need Phil to tell me that.

So, what happens when we worship Phil? He, his wife, and his two kids get cozy accommodations in a manmade burrow, with all the food, attention, and backscratches they could ask for. He is seen as a royalty in Punxsutawney, a benevolent king, held above all other animals. I think the truth of Phil is closer to that of a scheming dictator. Do not be swayed, dear reader, by Punxsutawney’s seemingly innocent tradition. For, beneath it, a great evil lies, snacking on all the bananas his malevolent heart desires.
