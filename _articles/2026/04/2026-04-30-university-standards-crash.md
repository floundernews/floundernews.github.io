When university standards skyrocketed near the beginning of the school year, all the grade 12 students were trying their hardest to get into a school of their choice. It was expected that applicants started their own charities to solve global hunger and knew how to speak in 12 languages. But as time passed, seniors began to grow tired and just wanted to get into any school that they could. Eventually this extreme competitiveness took a toll on the grade 12s, and in spectacular human fashion, they did the most human thing they could do. They gave up.

Since then, university populations have sunk 20% and universities couldn't find anyone who qualified for their standards. The average grade 12 student’s GPA plummeted from a 98% to a 54%. It seemed that the people in charge of these universities realized what was at stake. One Farvard trustee, Mr Dale says "I don’t understand why no one is able to satisfy our criteria of landing on the moon. This is ridiculous."

As years passed, standards slowly began to decrease. One university no longer requires a gold medal in the Olympics to apply, only needing “Top 10 Finalist”. Another removed having a Nobel Prize by age 5, changing it to age 10\. Although professors were reluctant for this change, it continued to catch on and become normalized. However, even after all these changes, many students still had lost all hope. One student, Fran Murr, went back to her family farm to teach her cows how to jump. Another student, Conan Vience, started working at a gas station.

It took a long time, but eventually universities realized it would be impossible to sustain their schools without people \- and that it really didn't matter if you could swim a mile in 3 minutes in order to learn accounting. Standards have at last hit the floor. After slowly lowering criteria one by one, removing medal after medal, skill after skill, all that was really left was being able to breathe.

Soon, everyone got into university: super seniors well past their time, people who steal candy for fun, and a whole circus\! Parents even enrolled their babies in hopes of getting them an early start. The halls slowly started crowding more and more. People who couldn't even do 3x3 were in Farvard. And it was all because they asked for too much. Professer Quint Err from Kale commented, "In my past 10 years of teaching, I have never had to teach someone old enough to be my dad and my own grandson in the same class."

Students who wanted a real education were rioting, because they were forced to sit next to toddlers and their moms while trying to learn. They were ready to leave, and so was everyone else. After all that desperation to prevent students from leaving, students proceeded to that exact thing. Once again, the halls of Kale were empty, Farvard was a graveyard for education, and Chrinceton lost a water fountain. Suffice to say, the human spirit is very good at quitting, and university standards will never be attainable.
